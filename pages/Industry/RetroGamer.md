Retro Gamer is a British magazine devoted entirely to classic video games, first launched in January 2004 [^1]. Over the past 20+ years it has grown from a niche quarterly into a monthly archive of gaming history, publishing high-quality retrospectives, developer interviews, and reader-favorite features on titles from the 1970s through early 2000s . 

The magazine's content and structure have continually evolved – from its early format under Live Publishing (complete with cover CD-ROMs of emulators) to later expansions under Imagine and Future plc [^2].

For a complete history of the magazine check out issue 257 which celebrates 20 years of the magazine by including a 31 page section all about the history of the magazine, including interviews with journalists who worked on it throughout the years.

# Interviews with Game Developers
One of Retro Gamer's hallmarks is its extensive interviews with the creators of classic video games. Nearly every issue features conversations with veteran developers, offering first-hand insights into the making of beloved games and the industry's early days. 

These interviews often explore how iconic titles were designed under technical constraints and the creative tricks used to overcome hardware limitations. The page numbers below refer to the printed magazine pages in issues 1-129 and 131-252. Issue 130 was not available in the scanned collection and has not yet been checked. Some features combine interviews with editorial history or first-person accounts. Issue 221 also includes a separately numbered Sonic Mini Companion; its interviews are labelled in the table.

# The Cover Discs
The first 18 issues came with cover CDs. When the magazine returned with issue 19 under a new publisher, its editorial announced that the monthly disc had ended and that the cover price was a pound lower [^3]. The discs paired games with emulators, PC remakes, creator tools, video and music. Each issue below has its own disc sleeve and a summary drawn from the magazine's printed guide or, for the incomplete issue 1 scan, the surviving sleeve [^4].

The images show the disc sleeves, not the magazine front covers. They come from the Centre for Computing History's collection; select a sleeve to see it at full size. The page numbers below are printed magazine pages, not PDF page numbers.

## Issue 1 - Special Collector's Disc
The surviving issue 1 PDF names a coverdisc guide at printed p. 92, but that page is absent from the scan. The surviving sleeve advertises more than 200 PC retro games and 75 emulators. Its mixture of playable games and emulation software established the disc as a second way to explore the systems discussed in the magazine.

## Issue 2 - Retro Gamer System
The sleeve advertises more than 100 retro PC games. The magazine also uses the disc as a teaching aid: its game-creation tutorial on p. 79 works with the included 30-day demo of Clickteam's Multimedia Fusion 1.5. Readers build a submarine game called *Sub-Attack* and can save project files, although the demo cannot export a standalone executable.

## Issue 3 - Gremlin's Greatest Hits
The guide on pp. 100-103 groups Gremlin games by original computer format and supplies matching emulators. It also includes scans of advertising and game inlays, making the CD a small archive of the company's presentation as well as its software. The guide says more than 250 games across seven formats; the sleeve advertises more than 200, so the two printed counts differ.

{% include article-figure.html src="/public/images/magazines/retro-gamer-coverdiscs/issue-03.jpg" alt="Cover CD sleeve for Retro Gamer issue 3, Gremlin's Greatest Hits" width="505" height="715" size="small" title="Issue 3 cover CD sleeve" caption="Sleeve scan from the [Centre for Computing History](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/cgi-bin/sitewise.pl?act=det&p=69529)." %}

## Issue 4 - Retro Collection
This mixed-media CD plays 18 retro-themed audio tracks in a standard CD player. In a computer drive it offers PC remakes, emulators, utilities and video material. The guide on pp. 104-107 explains the distinction between the audio session and computer files, as well as the Windows browser used to find and launch programs.

{% include article-figure.html src="/public/images/magazines/retro-gamer-coverdiscs/issue-04.jpg" alt="Cover CD sleeve for Retro Gamer issue 4, Retro Collection" width="1000" height="1409" size="small" title="Issue 4 cover CD sleeve" caption="Sleeve scan from the [Centre for Computing History](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/cgi-bin/sitewise.pl?act=det&p=69525)." %}

## Issue 5 - Retro Gaming Extravaganza
The guide on pp. 106-109 lists more than 50 PC retro games, 20 emulators, 18 original Commodore 64 games and demos, and four Commodore 64 music videos. It also includes GameBase 64, a database and launcher for exploring C64 software. This disc is a useful example of the magazine pairing modern remakes with original 8-bit files and tools.

{% include article-figure.html src="/public/images/magazines/retro-gamer-coverdiscs/issue-05.jpg" alt="Cover CD sleeve for Retro Gamer issue 5, Retro Gaming Extravaganza" width="1000" height="1418" size="small" title="Issue 5 cover CD sleeve" caption="Sleeve scan from the [Centre for Computing History](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/cgi-bin/sitewise.pl?act=det&p=69519)." %}

## Issue 6 - Beneath a Steel Sky
The full version of Revolution Software's adventure is the headline inclusion, alongside 20 further full games and playable demos. The printed instructions on pp. 106-107 show PC and Mac readers how to run *Beneath a Steel Sky* through ScummVM: first locate the game files, then add them in ScummVM's menu. The disc therefore preserves both the game data and the period's recommended compatibility layer.

## Issue 7 - Retro 50
The guide on pp. 106-107 describes 50 contemporary PC retro games spanning arcade, adventure and puzzle styles. It also includes more than an hour of video showing 160 ZX Spectrum games being completed, tied to the issue's *Over the Rainbow* feature. Superior Software and Trymedia demos give readers further games to sample.

{% include article-figure.html src="/public/images/magazines/retro-gamer-coverdiscs/issue-07.jpg" alt="Cover CD sleeve for Retro Gamer issue 7, Retro 50" width="507" height="718" size="small" title="Issue 7 cover CD sleeve" caption="Sleeve scan from the [Centre for Computing History](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/cgi-bin/sitewise.pl?act=det&p=69528)." %}

## Issue 8 - Graftgold Collection
The CD collects 50 Graftgold games across six formats, including *Paradroid*, *Uridium* and *Quazatron*. The guide on pp. 100-103 lists the original platforms and explains how to launch them with the bundled emulators. A separate video feature contributes more than 40 minutes of footage about the unreleased Konix Multi System, so this disc also preserves material about a cancelled machine.

{% include article-figure.html src="/public/images/magazines/retro-gamer-coverdiscs/issue-08.jpg" alt="Cover CD sleeve for Retro Gamer issue 8, Graftgold Collection" width="1000" height="1421" size="small" title="Issue 8 cover CD sleeve" caption="Sleeve scan from the [Centre for Computing History](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/cgi-bin/sitewise.pl?act=det&p=69527)." %}

## Issue 9 - Retro Remakes Collection
The disc carries all 76 entries in the 2004 Retro Remakes Competition and every entry in that year's Interactive Fiction Competition. The guide on pp. 100-103 explains how to search the disc and extract games supplied as archives. The two competitions place graphical remakes beside text adventures, documenting two active strands of hobbyist game development.

{% include article-figure.html src="/public/images/magazines/retro-gamer-coverdiscs/issue-09.jpg" alt="Cover CD sleeve for Retro Gamer issue 9, Retro Remakes Collection" width="1000" height="1402" size="small" title="Issue 9 cover CD sleeve" caption="Sleeve scan from the [Centre for Computing History](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/cgi-bin/sitewise.pl?act=det&p=69526)." %}

## Issue 10 - The Games Factory
Alongside 50 PC retro games and remakes, this disc includes the complete Home Version of Clickteam's The Games Factory. The guide on p. 96 introduces the browser, while a tutorial beginning on p. 98 uses the Storyboard, Level and Event editors to build a bat-and-ball game. The magazine says this edition is not time-limited or feature-restricted and includes a PDF manual.

{% include article-figure.html src="/public/images/magazines/retro-gamer-coverdiscs/issue-10.jpg" alt="Cover CD sleeve for Retro Gamer issue 10, The Games Factory" width="1000" height="1449" size="small" title="Issue 10 cover CD sleeve" caption="Sleeve scan from the [Centre for Computing History](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/cgi-bin/sitewise.pl?act=det&p=69516)." %}

## Issue 11 - Durell Big 40 Hits
The guide on pp. 100-105 presents more than 40 Durell games across seven formats, including *Saboteur*, *Turbo Esprit* and *Thanatos*. Bundled emulators allow readers to try the original versions on PC or Mac. The CD also includes recorded playthroughs of four Spectrum games: *Critical Mass*, *Saboteur*, *Thanatos* and *Turbo Esprit*.

{% include article-figure.html src="/public/images/magazines/retro-gamer-coverdiscs/issue-11.jpg" alt="Cover CD sleeve for Retro Gamer issue 11, Durell Big 40 Hits" width="1000" height="1411" size="small" title="Issue 11 cover CD sleeve" caption="Sleeve scan from the [Centre for Computing History](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/cgi-bin/sitewise.pl?act=det&p=69522)." %}

## Issue 12 - Llamarama
This collection accompanies the issue's Jeff Minter feature. Its guide on pp. 100-103 lists 65 Llamasoft games across seven formats, including *Hover Bovver*, *Gridrunner* and *Revenge of the Mutant Camels*. The magazine supplies emulators such as Spectaculator and blueMSX, then walks through launching the original files. It is a compact cross-platform view of one developer's 8-bit catalogue.

{% include article-figure.html src="/public/images/magazines/retro-gamer-coverdiscs/issue-12.jpg" alt="Cover CD sleeve for Retro Gamer issue 12, Llamarama" width="1000" height="1426" size="small" title="Issue 12 cover CD sleeve" caption="Sleeve scan from the [Centre for Computing History](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/cgi-bin/sitewise.pl?act=det&p=69515)." %}

## Issue 13 - Amiga Forever 6.0
The disc's central package combines the WinUAE and WinFellow emulators with Amiga ROM files, games, demos and utilities. The guide on pp. 106-108 explains installation and gives readers an entry point into Amiga emulation. More than 50 PC retro remakes appear alongside Amiga Forever, so the CD pairs a complete emulation package with a broader remake collection.

{% include article-figure.html src="/public/images/magazines/retro-gamer-coverdiscs/issue-13.jpg" alt="Cover CD sleeve for Retro Gamer issue 13, Amiga Forever 6.0" width="1000" height="1403" size="small" title="Issue 13 cover CD sleeve" caption="Sleeve scan from the [Centre for Computing History](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/cgi-bin/sitewise.pl?act=det&p=69524)." %}

## Issue 14 - The Ur-Quan Masters
The main attraction is the complete PC and Mac port of *Star Control II*, with speech and music. The guide on pp. 106-108 explains installation and offers a beginner's route into its space exploration and combat systems. Additional PC retro games remain accessible through the usual disc browser, making the full game the focus without occupying the CD alone.

{% include article-figure.html src="/public/images/magazines/retro-gamer-coverdiscs/issue-14.jpg" alt="Cover CD sleeve for Retro Gamer issue 14, The Ur-Quan Masters" width="1000" height="1418" size="small" title="Issue 14 cover CD sleeve" caption="Sleeve scan from the [Centre for Computing History](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/cgi-bin/sitewise.pl?act=det&p=69518)." %}

## Issue 15 - 105 Essential Emulators
The sleeve promises 105 emulators, while the guide on p. 106 describes more than 100 PC programs covering machines from the Sinclair ZX80 and Oric Atmos to later consoles. Some are installers and others are compressed archives, so the magazine explains how to search, extract and run them. This disc primarily preserves the emulation ecosystem rather than a single game's files.

{% include article-figure.html src="/public/images/magazines/retro-gamer-coverdiscs/issue-15.jpg" alt="Cover CD sleeve for Retro Gamer issue 15, 105 Essential Emulators" width="1000" height="1401" size="small" title="Issue 15 cover CD sleeve" caption="Sleeve scan from the [Centre for Computing History](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/cgi-bin/sitewise.pl?act=det&p=69521)." %}

## Issue 16 - The Elder Scrolls Arena
Bethesda supplied the complete first *Elder Scrolls* game rather than a demo. The guide on pp. 106-108 starts with installing *Arena* and DOSBox, then shows how to mount a drive and launch the DOS game from within the emulator. The ordinary Retro Gamer disc browser remains available for other programs on the CD.

{% include article-figure.html src="/public/images/magazines/retro-gamer-coverdiscs/issue-16.jpg" alt="Cover CD sleeve for Retro Gamer issue 16, The Elder Scrolls Arena" width="519" height="720" size="small" title="Issue 16 cover CD sleeve" caption="Sleeve scan from the [Centre for Computing History](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/cgi-bin/sitewise.pl?act=det&p=69523)." %}

## Issue 17 - Ultimate 2D Shoot-'Em-Up Collection
The magazine groups 50 homebrew PC shooters on this disc, covering vertical and horizontal styles. Its guide on pp. 92-93 discusses individual games including *Thunder Slash*, *Cloudphobia*, *G-Type* and *Noiz2sa*, showing how different creators reused and varied arcade shooter ideas. The collection documents contemporary independent development rather than original commercial ROM releases.

{% include article-figure.html src="/public/images/magazines/retro-gamer-coverdiscs/issue-17.jpg" alt="Cover CD sleeve for Retro Gamer issue 17, Ultimate 2D Shoot-'Em-Up Collection" width="1000" height="1394" size="small" title="Issue 17 cover CD sleeve" caption="Sleeve scan from the [Centre for Computing History](https://www.computinghistory.org.uk/cgi-bin/sitewise.pl?act=det&p=69514)." %}

## Issue 18 - The Last Ninja Trilogy
The final monthly CD contains the original 8-bit ROMs for the three *Last Ninja* games, supplied through an arrangement with Play It. The guide on pp. 90-93 explains how to open the files with emulators including WinVICE for Commodore 64 and ZX32 for ZX Spectrum. It also offers a walkthrough of the first two stages of the original game; issue 19 then announced that the coverdisc had ended.
